Redesigning how a fintech brand talks to its users

01

Research

Context

The problem in plain terms

The product worked. Users were completing transactions, but drop-off rates in the onboarding flow were high and support tickets pointed to confusion at every step. The brand had grown organically and it showed — inconsistent tone, mismatched visuals, and a UI built by engineers, not designers.

The brief was to rebuild trust with the user, starting from how the product looks and speaks.

User Research

Who we were designing for

We conducted 12 moderated interviews across two user segments — frequent senders and occasional users — alongside an analysis of 6 months of support data. What emerged was a clear split: power users wanted speed and control, while occasional users wanted reassurance and clarity.

Competitive Landscape

Where this product sat in the market

A structured audit of six direct competitors revealed a pattern: most fintech apps default to either sterile enterprise aesthetics or overcrowded dashboards. There was a clear white space for a product that felt personal and direct without sacrificing trust signals.

Competitor audit — visual and UX benchmarking

02

Design

Direction

From principles to pixels

Before opening Figma, we established three design principles to keep decisions grounded: Clarity first — no element earns its place without a clear purpose. Trust through structure — visual hierarchy should do the work, not copy. Speed for the confident — experienced users shouldn't be held back by flows built for first-timers.

Information Architecture

Restructuring the flow

The existing flow had 11 steps between landing and completing a transfer. After mapping every decision point and cutting anything that wasn't load-bearing, we got to 5. The key move was surfacing the rate before asking for any personal information — reversing the original order entirely.

Revised user flow — before and after

Exploration

Wireframes and early concepts

We ran three rounds of low-fidelity wireframes, testing each with a subset of the original interview participants. The first round validated the flow restructure. The second surfaced issues with how we were handling the rate display on smaller screens. The third was clean.

Lo-fi wireframe iterations — rounds 1 and 3

Visual Identity

Building the design system

The visual language was built from scratch. A considered typographic system and a tight grayscale palette. Every component was documented as it was built, so the handoff to engineering was a system, not a folder of screenshots.

Design system — color, type, and component library

Prototyping

Testing at fidelity

High-fidelity prototypes were built in Figma with working micro-interactions. Five usability sessions confirmed that the new flow performed significantly better on task completion and perceived trust. One pain point remained: users on older Android devices found the animation timing too fast. We tuned the easing curves accordingly.

Reflection

What I'd do differently

The research phase was strong. Where we lost time was in the first round of visual exploration — too many directions explored in too little depth. In future projects I bring stakeholders into the process earlier to align on aesthetic direction before any high-fidelity work begins.

The copy rewrite was underestimated in the original scope. It ended up being one of the highest-impact changes, and it deserved more time. I now treat content design as a first-class phase, not a finishing step.
